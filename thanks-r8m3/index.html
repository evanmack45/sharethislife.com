A one-page card to print and keep nearby

How to record a story chapter

Ten to twenty minutes is enough. You don’t need a special app — the voice memo app already on the phone will do.

Before you start

  • Ask if it’s all right to record, and say you’re having this turned into a written chapter for the family.
  • Sit somewhere quiet. Put the phone on the table between you, not at the far end.
  • On an iPhone, open Voice Memos and tap the red button. On most Androids, open Recorder (or Voice Recorder) and tap record.
  • When you’re done, stop and play a few seconds back so you know it captured their voice.
  • If you already have a recording — a voice memo, a video, a call — you can send that instead.

Ten good questions

You don’t have to ask all of them. Pick a few and let the conversation go where it goes.

  1. What did an ordinary Saturday look like when you were about ten?
  2. What food takes you straight back to being a kid, and who usually made it?
  3. What do you wish people knew about your parents that doesn’t come through in photographs?
  4. How did you know you were falling for someone — a particular moment, or a slow change?
  5. What was your first real job, and what do you remember about the first week?
  6. When life got heavy, who did you call, or where did you go?
  7. What did you believe at twenty-five that you see differently now?
  8. Is there a story about your life you hope someone in the family will keep telling?
  9. If we could spend one ordinary day together from any year of your life, which day would you pick?
  10. Is there something you’ve never been asked that you wish someone would ask you now?
